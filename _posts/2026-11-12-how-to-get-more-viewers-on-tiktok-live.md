## Why TikTok LIVE views are low

TikTok LIVE views are low when the stream is shown to fewer people. Viewers arrive when TikTok recommends the LIVE on the For You feed, when followers know your schedule, or when you send them with a post or a LIVE Event. The count stays small if the LIVE is not eligible to be recommended, if nobody knew you were starting, or if new viewers leave in the first moments.

This guide assumes you can already go LIVE. TikTok's Community Guidelines require you to be 18 or older to go LIVE, and the account has to stay within those rules. If the LIVE button is missing, start with access.

- **Not recommended** The LIVE falls short of For You feed standards, so TikTok shows it to fewer people.
- **Unattended or unoriginal** A recording, a blank screen, or a host who has stepped away gives viewers nothing live to watch.
- **No one was invited** The stream starts with no LIVE Event, no teaser, and no time followers can plan around.
- **The open is flat** New viewers get no greeting and no topic, so they move on.

## How TikTok shows a LIVE to viewers

A LIVE reaches people on two paths. One is recommendation. TikTok's [help article on viewer interaction](https://www.tiktok.com/live/studio/help/article/Boost-viewer-engagement/How-to-increase-viewer-interaction-and-engagement) says engaging content can help your LIVE appear on the For You feed.

It treats following, gifting, sharing, and commenting as high-value viewer actions. Those actions come from people who are already in the room. TikTok still decides who is shown the LIVE before they arrive.

The other path is the audience you already have. TikTok's [guide to reaching more viewers with a schedule](https://www.tiktok.com/live/studio/help/article/Before-you-go-LIVE/Learn-to-reach-more-viewers-with-a-schedule) tells creators to stream consistently, put the schedule on their profile, set a LIVE Event, and post a teaser video so people can tune in at the right time.

Followers can only join a LIVE they know is happening. If they never hear that you are on, fix the announcement. If new people never arrive, check whether the LIVE is eligible to be recommended, and watch what the first viewers do when they land.

## When a LIVE is not recommended

TikTok's [Community Guidelines on accounts and features](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/accounts-features) say LIVE creators have to keep a safe room. If a session includes content that violates those policies, TikTok may stop it. If a creator repeatedly shares content that falls short of For You feed standards, TikTok may restrict the visibility of their LIVE sessions, or limit certain LIVE features.

A LIVE with content that is not suitable for younger people is limited to viewers 18 and older. The host owns the room, including guests. TikTok says that if a guest shares content that is ineligible for the For You feed, the LIVE is removed from that feed.

A guest who breaks the rules can be removed, and the host can lose guest features for a while. Authenticity sits in the same rules. Pretending to be someone else, or streaming someone else's content without permission, can lead to restrictions.

### Recordings, blank screens, and a host who leaves

TikTok's [LIVE Studio community guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/live/studio/help/article/Before-you-go-LIVE/Community-Guidelines) describe the LIVEs that get limited viewership. Streaming previously recorded content is not recommended. A no-face effect can be read as low interaction, and the LIVE can be treated as a recording.

Stay present, with your face on camera, for the session you are hosting. Stuck frames and still images make a LIVE look recorded, so use a connection that keeps the picture moving. A blank screen, a QR code, or another platform's link should stay out of the frame.

If you have to leave, pause the LIVE. Reading comments in real time is the habit TikTok names for lowering the chance of limited viewership.

Do not stream movies, TV, pay-per-view sports, or another creator's videos, including by screen capture. TikTok prohibits that.

> **Tip** Pause the LIVE if you leave the chair. An empty frame is one of the things TikTok tells creators to avoid.

## Go LIVE on a schedule, and stay for the session

TikTok's schedule guide calls consistent LIVE streams the way to grow an audience and give people a reason to come back. Pick days and a start time you can keep on a normal week. Put that schedule on your profile, in words a stranger can understand.

A repeated slot becomes a habit for the people who already like the show. Plan a block of time you can host the whole way through, on the topic you announced. A stream that ends a few minutes after it starts gives a late arrival nothing to join.

TikTok's LIVE guidelines tell you to stay on screen for the duration, and to pause if you step away. LIVE analytics show whether that block held anyone. TikTok tracks how long you were LIVE and the average time viewers spent watching, and it says engaging, interactive sessions help people watch longer.

## Tell people before you go LIVE

A schedule on your profile is the standing notice. A LIVE Event and a teaser are the notice for this session. TikTok says to set the event in LIVE Studio before you go LIVE, to post a teaser that includes the event info, and to keep the schedule in your bio.

1. **Set a LIVE Event** Create it before you go LIVE, with the day, the time, and the topic of that session.
2. **Post a teaser** A short video that states the time and what you will do gives the event something to point at.
3. **Update your profile** Put the days you actually keep, and the usual topic, where a new visitor will see them.
4. **Share it once you are on** TikTok's LIVE Studio help says to share the LIVE from your profile, and to ask viewers to invite friends.
{: .steps}

### Write a title that matches the hour

Add a title before you start, and make it specific. For a gaming LIVE, TikTok says to put the game and the schedule in the title and in About Me, select the topic that matches the game, and turn the camera on. For any other LIVE, name the topic in plain words.

"Answering questions about night-shift cooking" tells a new viewer what the hour is. Choose a topic you can host for the whole block. TikTok tells gaming creators to pick a game they enjoy and a style they can repeat, and it tells lifestyle creators to stay inside a niche they care about.

Mention the theme again when someone new joins. TikTok says to have a theme for each LIVE and to bring it up as new viewers arrive.

## Keep the first minutes, then fill the room

TikTok's interaction guide puts the weight on the opening. Greet people as they join, thank gifters by name, and ask a question they can answer in chat. A short story that fits the theme gives a new person a reason to stay another minute.

Thank regulars, and still state the topic for anyone who just arrived. Keep overlays simple so they do not cover what people came to see. If you have a moderator, ask them to repeat the topic in chat.

Read comments out loud. TikTok's LIVE Studio guidelines tie that habit to a lower chance of limited viewership.

### Co-host, multi-guest, and LIVE Battles

A second voice changes who can find the LIVE. On LIVE Studio, co-host lets you invite up to three other LIVE creators onto a split screen. TikTok says co-hosting can bring in viewers from the other creator's audience.

Co-hosting is also how you start what TikTok calls a LIVE match, and what creators call a LIVE Battle. TikTok says that match gives viewers a competition to watch and increases participation. Agree on the topic before you go LIVE, so a new viewer can follow the conversation.

Multi-guest, on LIVE Studio, is audio. Guests talk while you stay on camera. Use it for a question, a regular viewer, or a moderator who can thank people for Gifts.

Keep the rules tight. You are responsible for what guests say and show. TikTok's Community Guidelines say ineligible content from a guest takes the whole LIVE off the For You feed.

## Read LIVE analytics, and skip viewer promises

After the LIVE, open LIVE analytics in LIVE Center. TikTok splits past performance into viewership, engagement, rewards, and activity, and you can look back from 7 days to 60. Viewership includes views, unique viewers, average watch time, and peak concurrent viewers, the most people in the room at once.

Engagement covers new followers, comments, likes, and shares. Activity covers how long you were LIVE and how many days you showed up. Traffic sources show whether people came from your followers or from discovery.

Sort past LIVEs by most viewed and see which topic held a room. Compare a LIVE people stayed in with one they left, and check that you started when your profile and teaser said you would. If one session collapsed, look for a restriction notice on that LIVE before you rebuild the format.
